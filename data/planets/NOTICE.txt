Downloaded 2026-11-14. Original 2K JPEG files are unmodified.
ASTRA maps these images onto spheres with simulated lighting and rotation.
Individual source URLs, byte sizes and SHA-256 hashes: sources.json

The provider describes these maps as based on NASA elevation and imagery,
with colors adjusted using Messenger, Viking, Cassini and Hubble photographs.
Its Earth map combines geodata and NASA Blue Marble imagery.
The provider notes that unmapped gaps contain fictional fill, colors are
more saturated, and the Earth vegetation/snow coverage is seasonal.
These are processed visual reconstructions, not raw or live observations.

Mercury: 2k_mercury.jpg -- surface map.
Venus: 2k_venus_atmosphere.jpg -- visible cloud appearance, not radar ground.
Earth: 2k_earth_daymap.jpg -- daylight land/ocean map.
Mars: 2k_mars.jpg -- surface map.
Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune: corresponding 2k_<planet>.jpg -- atmospheric
appearance; these do not show a solid surface or current cloud positions.
Detail maps added for rendering:
- 2k_earth_nightmap.jpg: processed night-side lights, not current city lights.
- 2k_earth_clouds.jpg: cloud opacity layer, not current weather.
- 2k_earth_normal_map.png: terrain normals; converted from the provider's
  original 2k_earth_normal_map.tif to PNG, preserving pixel dimensions.
- 2k_earth_specular_map.png: ocean reflectivity; converted from the provider's
  original 2k_earth_specular_map.tif to PNG, preserving pixel dimensions.
- 2k_saturn_ring_alpha.png: radial ring color/opacity, mapped to an annulus.
- 2k_sun.jpg: solar appearance map used in enhanced colors, not a true-color
  photograph of the visible photosphere or current solar activity.
These detail maps have the same provider and CC BY 4.0 license as above.
Original and converted hashes and processing notes are in sources.json.
All moons remain ASTRA's separate visual approximations.

Earth/Saturn axial tilt references:
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/
https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/facts/
Approximate polar/equatorial shape ratios use the cube of the published
mean/equatorial radius ratio, rounded for illustration:
https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html
The displayed axis orientations are illustrative, not date-specific.
Atmospheric single scattering, cloud/ring shadows, night-light intensity, ring
scattering and exposure are visual approximations. HDR bloom represents a subtle
camera response; it is not gas surrounding planets.

Generated exoplanets continue to use ASTRA's procedural textures.
